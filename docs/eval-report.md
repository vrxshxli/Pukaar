## How this was measured

- Ground truth comes from the generator: each message knows its family, kind, true priority and stated fields. True priority uses a separate rubric in `generate_flood_night.py`, not Triage's scoring rules.
- A simulated officer approves dispatches, pre-positioning and bulletins 1 minute after they appear. For 'possible duplicate' cards it uses the ground truth, standing in for an officer reading the raw messages; the automatic-only merge row shows the score without that help.
- Volunteers accept tasks after 1 minute and confirm the OTP 4 minutes after arriving.
- The baseline sends the three boats to every request message in arrival order. It adds no typing time and ignores rumours entirely, which makes it look better than a real control room. Supplies requests go on boats because nothing separates them.
- 'Time to assign' runs from a family's first message to the first approved dispatch (Pukaar) or the moment a boat takes the job (baseline).
- Field accuracy counts a field as correct when it matches what the message actually says, including 'not stated'. A people count that Listen marks as implied counts as not stated.
- Caution on field accuracy: the generator builds messages from phrase templates, and the rules extractor was written by the same team with the same vocabulary in mind, so rules-mode field accuracy here is an upper bound. Real messages are messier (typos, mixed scripts, missing words) and will score lower.
- Merging and timing results depend on how crowded the ward is: this night has about 70 families on nine lanes, so many unrelated families share a lane and landmark, which is the hard case for merging.
